The Problem

Course registration leaves students to manually cross-reference transcripts against prerequisites, guess whether a schedule fits, and judge course relevance to their career with no explainable reasoning and no way to adjust what matters to them.

  • No ranking by actual career relevance
  • No visibility into prerequisite / schedule conflicts before it's too late
  • Zero explanation — recommendations, where they exist, are a black box

Our Approach

A content-based, multi-criteria recommender. Every course and every career target carries the same 5-axis skill vector (ML · Statistics · Systems · Algorithms · Practice). Each eligible course is scored live against the student's actual transcript-derived skills, declared goal, interests, schedule and cost — then explained in plain language.

Transcript→ Skill Vector→ 5 Subscores→ Weighted Match %→ Plain-Language Reason

Five Explainable Subscores

  • Goals — skill-vector similarity to the career target, boosted by stated interests.
  • Gap — weighted by how far current skills fall short of the target; closing a bigger gap scores higher.
  • History — halved if a checkable prerequisite isn't confirmed on the transcript.
  • Schedule — discounted if the course eats an outsized share of the weekly time budget.
  • Cost — a fixed credit-value score.

Match % = Σ(subscore × weight) / Σweight — weights are student-adjustable sliders, normalised on every read.

Key Features

  • Explore — filterable, sortable by match / rating / workload.
  • Compare — two courses side by side across schedule and fit.
  • Progress — requirement completion and GPA, derived live.
  • Steerable weights — sliders re-rank everything live.
  • Conflict detection — saved courses checked for overlap.

Conclusion

Learnix shows a recommender can be rigorous and legible — every ranking traces to a named, adjustable signal. No cold-start problem, no black box: a brand-new student gets reasoned recommendations from day one, and can steer the algorithm itself.
